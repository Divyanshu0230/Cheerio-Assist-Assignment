Prompts I kept, and the one I threw out

I used Cursor. This is the log of the prompts that changed the work, including the one I threw out. I am not pasting the whole thread.

This one changed the product

Read Cheerio’s public template guide, WhatsApp onboarding, workflow nodes, and pricing. List the failures an owner would ticket. Keep the facts you can point at. Do not invent ticket volumes.

That is why the five issues are tied to real rules: lowercase template names, a number that cannot already be on the WhatsApp app, Facebook admin, a workflow keyword and toggle, and the calls already included on Growth (4) and Pro (10).

This one changed the price

Build the cost sheet so every driver is an input. Cite a real model price. Put the person who maintains the playbooks in the bill. Then price a per-resolution fee and show the company result if adoption falls.

Tokens came out at about ₹0.06 a session. The ₹49 fee still loses to giving Assist away, because the tickets come back and the fee does not cover that person. I had a draft where the fee looked profitable. It was only looking at tokens. I rewrote that block.

This one became the prototype

One workspace, one failed Diwali send. The owner can apply a fix. Switching the category back to Utility has to disable the button. A second rejection stops the retry. Asking about an ₹840 debit must not touch the wallet. The ticket a person receives should already have the campaign id.

The workspace is Hotel Amar. I am the owner on the screen, not a made-up manager. The campaign is CAM-1842.

This one failed

What are Cheerio’s top 5 support queries, and what should the chatbot say? Give me the solution, the metrics, and a price per conversation.

I got a docs bot, a CSAT target, and a per-chat price. I also got a search blurb that called Meta’s India rates rupees. The card is in dollars, about $0.0118 for a marketing message. I dropped the chatbot, dropped CSAT, dropped the fee, and kept Meta’s message rates out of the model. Assist does not send those messages. The volume numbers became cells I can edit, instead of figures pasted into a paragraph.

What I did not delegate

The salary. The split between people setting up, people using it every week, and people who barely log in. The finish rate on each issue, especially counting only 10% of charge questions as resolved. The 70% adoption. The rule that month 1 has to move tickets by about 10% or I don’t add a second playbook. Those are judgments. They are labeled as inputs so someone can disagree without rebuilding the arithmetic.
